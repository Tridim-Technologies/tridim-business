# Discovery research kit

These blank templates operationalize the existing [validation plan](../VALIDATION_PLAN.md). Use them alongside its recruiting criteria, neutral interview guide, and decision rules; they do not replace the guide or claim that research has been conducted.

## Suggested sequence

1. Assign a respondent ID before the session. Record the market, segment, business-size band, and respondent role without recording the person's name or contact details.
2. Ask permission before taking notes or recording. A recording is optional; do not record without explicit permission.
3. Complete one [interview record](INTERVIEW_RECORD_TEMPLATE.md) for each conversation. Mark what was observed, recalled, expressed as an opinion, or remains unverified.
4. Use a [workflow map](WORKFLOW_MAP_TEMPLATE.md) for one recent, concrete customer-to-payment example. Capture exceptions and identify which records are authoritative at each step.
5. After multiple interviews, update the [cross-interview synthesis](CROSS_INTERVIEW_SYNTHESIS.md). Keep segment and geography comparisons explicit and preserve counter-evidence.

## Privacy and repository handling

This is a public repository. Commit the blank templates and non-identifying aggregate conclusions only. Do not commit completed interview records, respondent keys, recordings, contact details, customer or employee data, invoices, screenshots, or identifiable quotations. Keep completed records in a private, access-controlled location chosen by the project owner. If a participant shares sensitive material, stop copying it into notes and ask for an anonymized example instead.

Use respondent IDs such as `R001`; keep any mapping between IDs and people separate from interview records. If a quote or example could identify a person or business, paraphrase it or omit it from any shared synthesis. Remove unnecessary personal details before sharing research with collaborators.

## Evidence discipline

- **Observed:** directly watched a real task or reviewed an anonymized artifact with permission.
- **Recalled:** the participant described a specific past event but no artifact was reviewed.
- **Opinion:** preference, prediction, or general evaluation rather than a demonstrated event.
- **Unverified:** an assertion that still needs confirmation.

Evidence strength describes how directly the material supports a finding; it is not a measure of participant enthusiasm. Note disconfirming cases and missing evidence. The 20-interview target is a learning target, not a representative sample or a statistical demand estimate.
